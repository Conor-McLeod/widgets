Every transformer block has a feed-forward network (FFN) after attention. In the original
transformer it was two matrices with a ReLU between them, applied to each token on its own:

> FFN(x) = W<sub>2</sub> · ReLU(W<sub>1</sub>x)

LLaMA, PaLM, Mistral, Gemma and most of their successors use a different layer called **SwiGLU**
(Shazeer, 2020):

> FFN<sub>SwiGLU</sub>(x) = W<sub>2</sub> · ( Swish(Wx) ⊙ Vx )

So they made two changes: a smoother activation, and a second projection that gets multiplied
in elementwise. Each one is worth looking at separately.

## Swish: a soft ReLU

Swish<sub>β</sub>(x) = x · σ(βx) multiplies its input by a sigmoid of that same input. The sigmoid
acts as a soft on/off switch: close to 1 for large positive x, close to 0 for large negative x.
With β = 1 it's also called **SiLU**, and that's the version SwiGLU uses.

Drag β. At β = 0 the sigmoid is ½ everywhere and Swish is just the line x/2. As β grows the
switch sharpens, and by β ≈ 8 the curve is hard to tell apart from ReLU. GELU, the activation
in GPT-2 and BERT, sits almost exactly on Swish at β ≈ 1.7.

Two things to notice. Swish **dips below zero** for negative inputs (the minimum is about −0.278
at β = 1) and only flattens out further left, so it isn't monotonic. Turn on the slope view as
well: ReLU's gradient is exactly 0 for every negative input, while Swish's is small but not
zero, so a unit that has drifted negative can still be trained back.

## The gate: multiplying two directions

The bigger change is the ⊙. A classic hidden unit computes act(w·x): it projects the input onto
a single direction w and bends the result. Its output can only change as you move along w, so
over any 2D slice of input space it looks like a ramp with straight, parallel contours.

A SwiGLU unit has two weight vectors. It computes Swish(w·x), the **gate**, and multiplies it
by v·x, a plain linear projection. Where w·x is large and positive the gate is about w·x, so the
unit is close to the **product** (w·x)(v·x). That's a quadratic function of the input, which a
single ReLU unit can't produce.

Start on **ReLU** and drag w to see the ramp rotate. Then switch to **SwiGLU** and drag v. The
output now changes sign across the line v·x = 0, but only on the side where the gate is open.
**Bilinear** drops the activation altogether. Shazeer tested that variant too, and it did
nearly as well, which suggests the multiplication matters more than the exact choice of
activation.

## Keeping the parameter count fair

The gate costs a third matrix: W and V are both d × h, and W<sub>2</sub> is h × d. With the usual
hidden size h = 4d, SwiGLU would have 1.5× the parameters and FLOPs of the layer it replaces,
and that wouldn't be a fair comparison. So the hidden size is cut to two thirds:
3 · d · (⅔ · 4d) = 2 · d · 4d.

LLaMA then rounds h up to a multiple of 256 to keep the matrix shapes friendly for GPU kernels.
For d = 4096 that gives 11,008, the odd-looking number you'll find in LLaMA 7B's config.

Slide the hidden size to see how quickly the third matrix adds up. At equal cost, the gated
layer with fewer hidden units trains to lower loss. Shazeer's paper doesn't explain why. It
famously credits the result to "divine benevolence".
